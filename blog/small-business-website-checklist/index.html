Small Business Website Checklist: 15 Things to Get Right

Use this practical 15-point checklist to plan a trustworthy small-business website, improve mobile usability, make contact easy, and prepare pages for search.

A small-business website does not need dozens of pages or flashy effects to be useful. It needs to explain what you do, help the right visitor decide whether you are a fit, and make the next step obvious. Use this checklist before launch or during a redesign.

1. Make the first screen clear

Within a few seconds, a visitor should understand who you help, what problem you solve, and what they can do next. Write a specific headline instead of a vague phrase like “Welcome to our website.” Add one primary action such as “Request a quote” or “Book a consultation.”

2. Build the essential pages

  • Home: a concise overview, key benefits, proof, and a next step.
  • Services: what is included, who each service is for, and common limitations.
  • About: the people, experience, approach, and values behind the business.
  • Contact: a clear contact method and realistic response expectations.
  • Privacy: explain what information a form collects and how it is used.

3. Design for phones first

Check the site on a real phone. Text should be readable without zooming, buttons should be easy to tap, menus should not cover content, and forms should not require unnecessary typing. Test slow connections and avoid large decorative media that delays the useful content.

4. Make trust specific

Use real team details, accurate service descriptions, genuine testimonials with permission, and examples you are allowed to show. Do not invent client logos, reviews, certifications, years of experience, or performance results. If you are new, explain your process and show a clearly labelled demonstration project instead.

5. Make the contact path easy

Ask only for information needed to respond. Label every field, explain required fields, provide a useful success or error message, and test where submissions actually go. A form that looks good but silently fails is worse than a simple, honest contact link.

6. Cover the SEO foundations

  • Give each important page a unique, descriptive title and meta description.
  • Use a clear main heading and meaningful section headings.
  • Choose a preferred URL and use consistent canonical tags.
  • Link related pages with descriptive anchor text.
  • Keep an XML sitemap of canonical, indexable pages.
  • Use robots.txt to manage crawling carefully; do not use it as a substitute for access control.
  • Add structured data only when it accurately represents the page or business.

These steps help search engines understand your content; they do not guarantee indexing or rankings. See the technical SEO checklist for more detail.

7. Check speed and accessibility

Compress images, avoid unnecessary scripts, use semantic HTML, give meaningful images useful alt text, and ensure every interactive element works with a keyboard. Maintain visible focus indicators and adequate color contrast. Test the most important user journeys rather than relying only on a score.

8. Measure meaningful actions

Track relevant outcomes such as completed enquiries, booked calls, qualified leads, or purchases—not just page views. Use privacy-respecting analytics and disclose tracking where required. Establish a baseline before changing the design so you can compare results fairly.

9. Launch with a repeatable review

  1. Test all navigation and external links.
  2. Submit a test enquiry and verify where it arrives.
  3. Check mobile layouts and keyboard navigation.
  4. Confirm HTTPS, canonical URLs, sitemap, and robots rules.
  5. Review spelling, contact details, and privacy language.
  6. Set a reminder to review outdated content each quarter.

What should you fix first?

Prioritize broken contact paths, unclear service information, mobile usability, and inaccurate business details before cosmetic refinements. Then improve page content based on real customer questions and the search terms people use to find your service.
